# AppShellLayout

The complete app chrome: a collapsible, branded **left column** (logo,
navigation, pinned footer), a three-region chrome bar above the page content,
and optionally a **second column on the right**. Apps import this template and
fill its slots — the frame is **never** rebuilt in the app.

**One shell, adapted per context.** Every view of an app is this one
`AppShellLayout`; what changes between views is what goes into the slots, not
which frame renders them. The overview has **no chrome bar**
(`bar={false}`): navigation and search („Suchen", a palette) live in the left
column, and the page brings its own heading. A topic view has a bar with the
breadcrumb (`pageLabel`) and its controls (`headerActions`) — see „The chrome
bar" below.

The overview's other two pages are the same shell with another page and
another current row (fixture prop `section`): „Geteiltes Wissen" — topics
shared with you and public topics you subscribed to — and „Entdecken", the
catalog of public topics with „Abonnieren".

## Two contexts, one shell

The stories are modelled on the two call sites of the reference consumer,
JLURAG (`web/src/components/`), with neutral content. Both are the same
component with a different fill; the shared story fixture that renders them is
`src/test/fixtures/app-shell.tsx`.

- **Colour scheme, contrast, accent and Style are not in the bar.** They live
  behind the user menu: „Einstellungen" opens a `SettingsDialog` whose
  „Darstellung" section is `AppearanceSettings`, the only place they are
  switched. „Templates/Settings → User Settings" walks that route.
- **Page content is a content template**, hung in as `children` — the shell
  brings the chrome and the one `<main>`, the template brings the page's
  heading. A table or a form page (`TableLayout`, `FormLayout`) goes in like
  the grid.

- **An item's settings open in a settings window.** The gear of a topic opens
  „Thema-Einstellungen", a `SettingsDialog` whose sections hold the rights
  per role (a `Table`), the members and the version history. Every settings
  window of the app is on its own page: „Templates/Settings".

## Slots & Props

| Slot / Prop | Type | Required | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `logo` | `ReactNode` | ✅ | Brand block — in the left column's header row **and** in the narrow top bar. |
| `nav` | `ReactNode` | ✅ | Navigation content — `<NavItem>` rows (with `asChild` for router links), a `SidebarPanel` around them, or a whole panel like the topic view's history. Lands in a `<nav aria-label={navLabel}>`; a `SidebarPanel` child brings its own insets. |
| `sidebarFooter` | `ReactNode` | — | Pinned foot of the left column: the user menu. |
| `navLabel` | `string` | — | Accessible name of the `<nav>` landmark **and** of the column around it (default „Hauptnavigation"). |
| `pageLabel` | `ReactNode` | — | Current location — headline-sized and bold, but a `<p>` and **not** a heading, on the left of the bar. Optional: omitted, the template renders **no element** for it. Truncates when the bar is short. |
| `search` | `ReactNode` | — | **Deprecated (0.93.0).** A field in the bar's centre. No view uses one any more — JustRAG searches through „Suchen" in the column, which opens a palette; still rendered for existing call sites, and to be removed. |
| `headerActions` | `ReactNode` | — | Chrome controls at the **right** end of the bar — an icon button, a menu, several, or nothing. The template renders no control of its own there. Below `lg` this region sits next to the logo in the narrow bar. |
| `leftOpen` | `boolean` | ✅ | Left column expanded (`true`) or the rail (`false`). |
| `onLeftOpenChange` | `(isOpen: boolean) => void` | ✅ | The toggle was pressed; receives the **requested** state. |
| `collapseLabel` | `string` | — | Name of the toggle while expanded (default „Navigation einklappen"). |
| `expandLabel` | `string` | — | Name of the toggle while collapsed (default „Navigation ausklappen"). |
| `leftWidth` | `number` | — | Expanded width of the left column in px. Omitted, `AppShell`'s 256 default applies. |
| `leftResize` | `AppShellPanel["resize"]` | — | Makes the left column draggable (`minWidth`, `maxWidth`, `onWidthChange`, `label` — all or nothing). Omitted, no separator. |
| `rightPanel` | `AppShellPanel` | — | Second column on the right, passed whole (the shape `AppShell.left`/`.right` take), `resize` included. Omitted, no landmark and no rail. |
| `showRight` | `boolean` | — | `false` takes `rightPanel` out of the **desktop** arrangement **without** touching its `isOpen`, and is ignored below `md`. Forwarded to `AppShell`. Hide a column per view with this instead of dropping `rightPanel` (that destroys its collapse state). |
| `notice` | `ReactNode` | — | The system banner, typically `Alert layout="banner"`: the frame's first row, full width over the columns, above the bar, in both arrangements. Pushes the frame down. Omitted, nothing renders. |
| `noticeLabel` | `string` | — | Accessible name of the `region` around `notice` (default „Systemmeldung"). |
| `status` | `ReactNode` | — | A floating status message, typically `Alert layout="pill"`: top centre of the main area, over the page (which does not move), stays while the page scrolls. Omitted, nothing renders. |
| `mobileTabs` | `MobilePaneTab[]` | ✅ | The areas of the narrow arrangement, each with `pane: "left" \| "main" \| "right"`. There is no tab bar: the ids name what is open (navigation drawer, sources sheet, neither). |
| `activeMobileTab` | `string` | ✅ | Id of the current tab — the **only** input that decides which area shows below `md`. |
| `onMobileTabChange` | `(id: string) => void` | ✅ | A drawer or the sheet was opened or closed; carries the area to show. |
| `mobileTabBarLabel` | `string` | — | Deprecated and unused: the narrow arrangement has no tab bar. |
| `sidebarNarrowAction` | `ReactNode` | — | The navigation's primary action below `md` („Neuer Chat"): the end of the drawer's bottom row, beside the footer in its short form. |
| `children` | `ReactNode` | ✅ | Page content — a content template (`GridLayout`, `SectionedGridLayout`, `DashboardLayout`, `TableLayout`, `FormLayout`, …) or a `ChatStage`. |

## The chrome bar

Only a view that needs one has a bar — in JustRAG the topic view: the
breadcrumb „Mein Wissen / ‹topic›" as `pageLabel`, the topic's gear and the
sources toggle at the end. `bar={false}` leaves it out, and nothing is
reserved: the page starts at the frame's top.

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithoutChromeBar} />

- **`pageLabel` on the left, `headerActions` on the right.** The label is a
  `<p>` (or a breadcrumb), not a heading, and truncates when the bar is short.
- **The bar is 56px high**, whatever it holds. That is a contract outside
  this repo: JustRAG places its toasts at `top: 68px` (= 56 + 12) under this
  edge. The topic-view stories assert it.
- **The bar takes the column gutter: `px-gutter`, 24px**, not the page
  measure of a `Container` — it is chrome between two columns, not page
  content. `TopicView` measures the label's distance from the history
  column against `--spacing-gutter` from the CSSOM.
- **No central search.** The `search` slot is deprecated (see the props): a
  search is a row of the column that opens a palette.

### `headerActions` — the right-hand position belongs to the app

The template renders **no** control of its own there. What goes in is the
app's decision:

- **One element** (the topic's gear) sits right-aligned.
- **Several** come as a fragment and stand in a row (`gap-2`).
- **Empty** means no box in the DOM — the overview context's case.

**The shell needs no `ThemeProvider`**: neither `AppShellLayout` nor
`AppShell` reads the theme. The appearance controls belong to the settings
window behind the user menu (see „Two contexts, one shell").

## The left column

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithCollapsibleColumns} />

```tsx
const [leftOpen, setLeftOpen] = useState(true);   // or URL, context, localStorage

<AppShellLayout
  logo={logo}
  nav={nav}
  sidebarFooter={userMenu}
  leftOpen={leftOpen}
  onLeftOpenChange={setLeftOpen}
  collapseLabel={t("collapseNavigation")}   // optional, the default is German
  expandLabel={t("expandNavigation")}
  mobileTabs={TABS}
  activeMobileTab={tab}
  onMobileTabChange={setTab}
  mobileTabBarLabel={t("switchArea")}
>
  {page}
</AppShellLayout>
```

- **The column renders its toggle itself**, in the header row on the edge
  that faces the content — the template brings **no** control of its own here
  and opens no slot. The toggle is the only control that survives collapsing,
  and it carries `aria-expanded` + `aria-controls` onto an `id` only
  `SidePanel` knows (`useId`, per mount). A slot could not reach that `id`.
- **The state belongs to the app.** There is deliberately no `defaultOpen`: a
  column width is exactly the kind of value an app stores per user.
- **Collapsed is the rail** (60px, `SIDE_PANEL_RAIL_WIDTH`). The same `nav`
  node moves into it — `NavItem`s shrink to their icon form, and a
  `nav` that renders differently in the rail reads `useSidebarCollapsed()`
  (the overview's rows drop their `SidebarPanel`, the topic view's history
  becomes a `SidebarRail`).
- **The rail mirrors the expanded column's rows**, so every control keeps its
  height across a collapse:

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.CollapsedRailKeepsVerticalPositions} />

  The story measures the overview context's composition: the rows in a
  `SidebarPanel` with no title, `head` or `nav` when expanded, bare in the
  rail. Such a panel renders no head, and its list carries the same 16px top
  inset as `AppShellLayout`'s own `p-4` nav wrapper and the rail — so bare
  rows and a head-less panel both start 16px below the chrome row.
- **Both label props are translatable** — the defaults are German.

## Second column on the right

`rightPanel` takes a whole `AppShellPanel` instead of six flat props: it is
exactly the shape `AppShell` itself takes, so there is no second vocabulary
for the same thing. `content`, `header`, `footer`, `label`, `isOpen`,
`onOpenChange`, `width` (default 256), `expandLabel`, `collapseLabel`,
`collapsedPreview` and `resize`. The topic context above
(`TopicView`) is the case: sources on the right, with a `collapsedPreview`
rail of one icon per source.

## System messages

The more serious the problem, the more prominent the message. The frame has
two places for a system state; the app picks one by severity.

**`notice` — outage or maintenance.** The frame's **first row**: top of the
viewport, **full width** (over the side columns too), above the chrome bar, in
both arrangements. It is in the flow, so it pushes the whole frame down and
covers nothing, and it never scrolls away. It is wrapped in a named `region`
(`noticeLabel`, default „Systemmeldung") so landmark navigation reaches it.
Hang in an `Alert` with `layout="banner"`: the tone's **solid** colour, no
radius, a slim centred row (36–40px for one line), „**Titel** · Text Aktion"
on one line that wraps on a narrow screen. Red (`error`) with
`live="assertive"` (`role="alert"`) — rare by design.

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithSystemNotice} />

**`status` — reduced operation.** High load, a degraded service. A small
`Alert` with `layout="pill"` floats at the **top centre of the main area**,
over the page, which neither moves nor reflows; it stays while the page
scrolls. The tone's container fill, a tinted border, the overlay shadow, as
wide as its text. `live="polite"` (`role="status"`), usually closable.

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithSystemStatus} />

The frame renders the slots; whether a message can be closed, and where that
is remembered, is the app's decision — `Alert`'s `onDismiss` plus the app's
own storage. One-off failures of a single request belong in a `Toast`, and an
error about one element (a message, a source row) belongs inline at that
element — not here.

## Native shell (Electron)

With `<html data-shell="native">` the side columns let the window's OS
material through, and with `<html data-titlebar="inset">` the top row drags
the window and keeps clear of the window controls. Both are set by the host;
without them the frame is unchanged. It is this component, not a second
layout: the app renders the same `AppShellLayout` on the web and in the
window. Its stories and the host's setup are on the page „Native shell".

## Resizable columns

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithResizableColumns} />

```tsx
// The widths belong to the app. `usePersistedWidth` is the optional hook
// that keeps them per device — a `useState` does the job just as well.
const [leftWidth, setLeftWidth] = usePersistedWidth("myapp.kb.leftWidth", {
  defaultWidth: 320, minWidth: 150, maxWidth: 600,
});
const [rightWidth, setRightWidth] = usePersistedWidth("myapp.kb.rightWidth", {
  defaultWidth: 320, minWidth: 300, maxWidth: 800,
});

<AppShellLayout
  logo={logo} nav={history} leftOpen={leftOpen} onLeftOpenChange={setLeftOpen}
  leftWidth={leftWidth}
  leftResize={{
    minWidth: 150, maxWidth: 600,
    onWidthChange: setLeftWidth,
    label: t("resizeHistory"),
  }}
  rightPanel={{
    ...sources,
    width: rightWidth,
    resize: {
      minWidth: 300, maxWidth: 800,
      onWidthChange: setRightWidth,
      label: t("resizeSources"),
    },
  }}
  mobileTabs={TABS} activeMobileTab={tab} onMobileTabChange={setTab}
  mobileTabBarLabel={t("switchArea")}
>
  {page}
</AppShellLayout>
```

- **It is the library's one `ResizeHandle`** — no second mechanism.
- **`resize` is an object, all or nothing.** As four loose optionals „handle
  without bounds" would be expressible — a `separator` without
  `aria-valuemin`/`-valuemax` is no APG splitter any more. `width` stays
  beside it, because it exists without dragging too (and has a default that
  the bounds deliberately do not have: those are the app's decision).
- **The arrow keys are mirrored per side**: `→` widens the *left* column, `←`
  the *right* one. The key moves the separator, `aria-valuenow` reports the
  column. `Home`/`End` are minimum and maximum and therefore **not** mirrored.
- **No handle on a collapsed column** (the rail is a fixed 60px; a separator
  there would report a value with no visible effect) and **none below `md`**
  (one area fills the screen — there is nothing to drag the column against).
- **Omitted, there is no handle**: no separator, no `id` on the `<aside>`,
  default 256.
- **The handle takes no layout width** — the column's edge *is*
  `<main>`'s edge, and the 8px grab zone straddles it
  (`HandleHasNoLayoutWidth` measures it with the browser's hit-testing).

### Storing the width: `usePersistedWidth`

```tsx
import { usePersistedWidth } from "@ki4jlu/design-system";
```

- **The consumer passes the whole key.** The package assigns no namespace: a
  library-owned prefix would collide between the apps that share a device and
  an origin (JustRAG prefixes `justrag.`).
- **The components stay controlled.** The hook is the piece hung into
  `leftWidth` + `leftResize.onWidthChange` — no default in `AppShellPanel`.
  Whoever keeps the width elsewhere (URL, profile, own store) simply does not
  call it.
- **Read once per mount**, written on every set. **Clamped on read**, not on
  write: a stored value can be older than today's bounds, and unclamped the
  column would show a width the handle can never pull back into range.
- **Every storage access is guarded.** Even *reading*
  `window.localStorage` throws in Safari's private mode and with blocked
  cookies. A lost preference must never cost the view — the hook then falls
  back to plain state.
- **`storage` is injectable** — that is what the field is for: the tests run
  against an in-memory `Storage`, and the story fixture passes one per mount,
  so a test run leaves nothing behind.

## Responsive behaviour

- **from `md`** (768px): left column | main column, the right panel as a
  card over the main area, a page's `AppShellSidePanel` as an end column.
  `<main>` scrolls on its own. Where the frame gets tight, it gives way in
  this order: a side panel shrinks to its minimum (the main area keeps
  360px), the left column shows as its rail, and the card closes at the
  width where it would stop standing beside the page.
- **below `md`**: the bar and the page stay on screen. The navigation is a
  drawer from the left edge, opened by a toggle at the start of the bar; the
  right panel is a `BottomSheet`, opened by its toggle at the end of the bar;
  a shown side panel fills the frame. It is this component, not a second
  template — the stories at phone width are on „Templates/Mobile Shell".

- **Why in JavaScript and not with an `md:` utility.** Below `md` a column
  is not a narrower version of itself: it is a drawer with a different way in
  and out, and its collapse state — a wide screen's preference — is not read.
  The app still writes no breakpoint query: which id shows which area is data
  (`mobileTabs`).
- **Drawers are overlays**: the page behind is inert, Escape and the scrim
  close them, and the focus returns to the toggle.
- **One `<main>`**, in every arrangement.
- **Every node is mounted exactly once**, in every arrangement. There is no
  drawer that would render the nav a second time and duplicate every `id` in
  it.

## Do's & Don'ts

- ✅ **One `AppShellLayout` for every view of an app** — adapt the slots per
  context (overview, topic), never switch to a second frame.
- ✅ **Search lives in the column** — a „Suchen" row that opens a palette;
  the bar's `search` slot is deprecated.
- ✅ Put appearance (colour scheme, contrast, accent, Style) behind the user
  menu's „Einstellungen" (`SettingsDialog` + `AppearanceSettings`), not into
  the bar.
- ✅ Set the active state through `NavItem active` (sets `aria-current="page"`).
- ✅ Hang page content in as a **content** template (`GridLayout`, `SectionedGridLayout`,
  `DashboardLayout`, `FormLayout`, `TableLayout`, `ChatLayout`) or a
  `ChatStage`.
- ❌ **Nothing that brings its own chrome goes inside**, and `AuthLayout`
  stays outside the shell: `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §4 → „Page templates".
- ✅ `headerActions` takes **chrome**: small tools, a menu. No page content, no forms, no business logic.
- ❌ **No heading in `headerActions`.** It is a chrome position, not a way
  around the `pageLabel` rule. Pinned in
  `heading-owner.test.tsx` — with filled slots the template still delivers
  **zero** headings.
- ✅ **Omit** `pageLabel` when the content template already renders the page
  title.
- ❌ **No empty `pageLabel` as a placeholder** (`pageLabel={<span />}`, an
  `&nbsp;`). Omit the prop.
- ❌ Do not build your own column or top bar in the app, or re-skin the
  template through `className` — if something is missing, the template is
  extended here.
- ❌ **Do not** compose `AppShell` + `SidePanel` by hand just to get a second
  column or a collapse toggle.
- ❌ **No drag mechanics of your own** beside `leftResize` /
  `rightPanel.resize` (a `pointermove` listener in the app, a handle made of
  your own markup): `ResizeHandle` owns the interaction, the app owns the
  number.
- ❌ **No collapse button of your own** in `logo` or `headerActions`: the
  column renders its toggle itself, with `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`. Two
  toggles would be two truths.
- ❌ No business logic handed in: routing and active state live in the
  injected `NavItem`s, not in the template.
